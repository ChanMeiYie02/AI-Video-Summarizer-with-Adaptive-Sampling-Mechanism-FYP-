-> The Quantized Version (4-bit - Previous): To save paper space (VRAM), the book is printed in a highly compressed code. Every time the GPU wants to read a word, it has to stop, translate the code back into plain language, and then process it. Even though the book is small, this constant translation slows the computer down.
-> The Unquantized Version (16-bit - Current): The book is printed in regular, plain language. It takes up slightly more space on the desk, but the GPU can read it directly at maximum speed without translating. Since your graphics card has a huge desk (24 GB of VRAM), there is plenty of room, so the size of the book is not an issue.
-> Conclusion: The extra work the GPU does to "uncompress" the 4-bit code makes it slower than just running the raw 16-bit model.

Why the Result Was Not Good
Multimodal LLMs like Gemma-4 rely on sensitive visual and auditory projection layers to map images/audio into the text space. Quantizing these projection weights to 4-bit completely broke the alignment, causing the model to:

Ignore actual PowerPoint slide frames and speech inputs.
Degenerate into repetitive hallucinated training templates (Donald Trump, red and black plaid shirts, IPTV logos, collage of female faces).
Reverting to unquantized precision (FP16/BF16) restores the model's sensory perception. Since your RTX A5500 has 25.75 GB of VRAM, the 2B model fits comfortably without quantization.
