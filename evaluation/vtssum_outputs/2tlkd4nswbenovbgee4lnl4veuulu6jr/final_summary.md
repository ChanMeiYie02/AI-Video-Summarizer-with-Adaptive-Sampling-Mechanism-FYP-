# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The presentation began by introducing a project funded by the Italian Government and Department for Research, involving the Eye Care Center and a PhD student, setting the stage for a discussion on the challenges of managing big data. The speakers contrasted an optimistic vision of big data use with a realistic view, emphasizing that simply including data in proposals is insufficient; the focus must shift to extracting real value through techniques like data mining and exploratory analysis.

The core objective of the project, the Wipac virtual Power Operating Center, is to address challenges faced by small renewable energy producers in Italy, who struggle with high penalties when selling directly to the market. To mitigate these risks, the main goal is to develop accurate forecasting methods for renewable energy production. Since producers face severe financial penalties for inaccurate daily output declarations, the system was designed to provide precise forecasts with an accuracy of up to 10% to minimize financial exposure.

To achieve this, the team gathered and processed historical and real-time data, including numerical weather predictions, utilizing sensors that captured data on altitude and sun position. This data was stored efficiently using a Hadoop-based structure. Data preparation involved normalizing measurements to handle sensor failures and biases, and incorporating advanced statistical methods, such as temporal and partial autocorrelation, to understand data correlations.

The predictive model was developed using neural networks, specifically the ProPlus algorithm with backpropagation, applying documentation statistics and the Lisa technique. The learning phase involved testing two methods to predict hourly and daily energy outputs. The input data relied on geographic coordinates and temperature, with the analysis confirming that specific data types, particularly temperature, are crucial for accuracy.

The team initiated testing using data collected from Sun Electrics, utilizing 77,131 days of data. The results were encouraging, showing that the inclusion of spatial and temporal features significantly improves forecast accuracy, especially in daily settings. The team noted that their model performance remained below the 10% error limit. The next steps involve incorporating autocorrelation measures and testing the model on different renewable energy data types. Looking forward, the team plans to establish a European consortium to submit a proposal for funding in early 2015.
