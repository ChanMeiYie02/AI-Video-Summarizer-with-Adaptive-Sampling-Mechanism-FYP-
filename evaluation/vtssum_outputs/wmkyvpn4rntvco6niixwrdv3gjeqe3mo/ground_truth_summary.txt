Sampling is the most important part of each procedure, as if we are sampling samples in our own way, we cannot repair this in the later stages of the procedure. We can sample for instance, water sediments, soil, biological materials like grass, lichens or food items. The most important thing is that we take representative sample for the purpose we want to achieve. For water samples, it is the best to filter them right after the sampling and preserve them by lowering pH to acidic by adding acid. We did that. Usually we are dealing with very low activity concentrations and 1st requisite is to pre concentrate samples. This can be done mostly by coprecipitation of radionuclides or evaporation of samples. For biological materials we have to divide or sample to specific organs or parts depending on the purpose of the analysis. Fish can be divided to muscle bounce, liver and spleen, plants on leaves, shots and roots. This is advisable to do as soon as possible after sampling or store samples frozen. In which cases we usually use freeze, drink or drank at temperatures lower than 90 degrees centigrade's. Then we homogenize the sample by grinding, chopping, milling, sieving, mixing and finally we take subsample for further analysis steps, usually by hand quartering. Next is to reduce sample mass and remove organic material. The ashing in furnace. Usually at temperatures between 500 and 650 degrees centigrade's. Soil or sediment samples with dry to remove water from them. The most volatile are polonium 210 and let 210, in which cases usually use freeze drying or dying at temperatures lower than 90 degrees.
