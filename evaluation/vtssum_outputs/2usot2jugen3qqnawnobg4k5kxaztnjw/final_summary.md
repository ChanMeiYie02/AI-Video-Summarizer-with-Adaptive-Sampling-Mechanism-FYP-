# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The presentation begins by establishing the framework for transmedia design, defining it as storytelling that travels across platforms in a platform-appropriate and audience-dependent manner, differentiating it from simple cross-media by requiring functional dependence on multiple platforms. Designers are characterized as hybrids who combine knowledge from art and psychology, focusing on clear storytelling and deep emotional investment to guide audiences. The discussion explores content critique, strategies for user participation—ranging from full co-creation to passive consumption—and methods for scaling projects by focusing on initial testing and audience reaction.

The segment then transitions to practical application with a focus on social impact. A key project, the "Eagle project," addressed stigmatization related to sexual, physical, and lexical abuse. The team researched stigmatized groups in Slovenia, concluding that existing psychiatric institutions were ineffective. Their solution was to use the project as a platform for dialogue and knowledge delivery. This work was further explored through narrative modification, specifically altering the Brothers Grimm story to address gender stereotypes, and through a multi-faceted exhibition featuring interactive elements and discussions on social topics.

Further projects investigated themes of migration, exploring the definition of belonging and researching diverse groups, while another effort focused on love through an exhibition featuring artists discussing their experiences. The team utilized technology to digitize artwork, allowing viewers to access explanatory video clips, and engaged with elderly users to explore the interaction between technology and people.

The focus then shifts to media strategy, involving the development of a documentary about a Slovenian figure, culminating in a strategic pivot: instead of traditional promotion, they delivered the content as a silent film accessible via a mobile podcast to foster co-creation through a unique "boot."

The presentation concludes by exploring future concepts, focusing on designing human interactions and cities driven by emerging technologies. The team examined the evolution of human connection, tracing the shift from basic data sharing to complex sensory data, and ultimately proposing an "aura economy" where individuals earn reputation points through reciprocity. This vision culminates in a call for a sustainable future and a reflection on personal connections, prompting a final invitation for audience questions.
