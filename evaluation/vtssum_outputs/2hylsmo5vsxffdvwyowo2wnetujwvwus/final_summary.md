# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The discussion begins by defining colorization, noting that polarization is the inverse of colorization, allowing a grayscale image to be converted back to its original colorized form. The methodology for this task involves defining polarization for both quantitative evaluation and qualitative assessment. Before developing an automatic colorization algorithm, the process focuses on analyzing the image by incorporating both low-level and high-level cues—such as identifying elements like grass, trees, and the sky—to build confidence in color assignments.

The primary motivation for this work is the development of an efficient automatic algorithm for colorizing old black and white photographs, aiming to provide economic benefits to the SEC, and to use Asian proxies as a visual understanding model. To achieve this, a convolutional neural network (CNN) model is designed, leveraging a task-based classifier to integrate semantic knowledge. The architecture employs a hyper-architecture that minimizes the distance between low-level features and the specific task. To handle the ambiguity inherent in colorization, the model predicts histograms to capture multi-modality, defining the color space using Hue, chroma, and lightness, and generating the final RGB image by sampling these predictions.

A key technical innovation introduced to manage the memory footprint of the hyper columns is the use of sparse sampling combined with direct bilinear interpolation. This method exploits the high correlation between hyper columns, enabling the training of larger images and comfortable batch sizes. This approach significantly improved quantitative colorization measurements compared to previous methods.

The model's performance demonstrated competitive quantitative results. A key finding was that running colorized images through a classifier substantially improved accuracy, demonstrating the robustness of the model by effectively colorizing objects that fall outside standard ImageNet classes. While the technique handles conservative predictions effectively, limitations such as inconsistent colors and color bleeding were noted. Furthermore, the research explored using colorization for self-supervision, confirming that training from scratch is feasible. The colorized model was subsequently applied to semantic segmentation, and initializing the segmentation network with the colorization model helped bridge performance gaps. Ultimately, the study concluded by presenting a fully automatic colorization model with state-of-the-art results, highlighting the efficiency of the sparse sampling training method as a promising proxy task for visual learning.
