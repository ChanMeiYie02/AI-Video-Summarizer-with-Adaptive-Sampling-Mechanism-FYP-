# Final Multimodal Video Summary

This research focuses on analyzing Greek Parliament data to understand the evolution of political and policy agendas and how members react to events. The methodology begins with the collection and digitization of data from over 4,356 plenary sessions, encompassing more than one million speeches.

The raw data was transformed into a structured XML corpus and enriched through preprocessing, including annotations such as speaker names, political affiliation, and speech timing. To track monthly dynamics, the data was organized using one-month windows. Dynamic topic modeling, inspired by Green Growth concepts, was then applied to identify semantically similar topics within these windows, resulting in 90 dynamic topics.

A key contribution of this work is the creation of a platform that enables insightful navigation of this analyzed data. This platform allows users to explore topics, time periods, and user satisfaction levels. Users can visualize details for each dynamic topic, including descriptive terms, associated regions, frequency, and the most prominent associated words, such as how "memorandum" relates to "negotiation" and "Eurozone." Furthermore, the platform includes charts detailing the contribution of political parties and electoral districts to specific topics, allowing for a more perceptive monitoring of the political agenda.

The project’s overall contribution is twofold: first, the successful digitization of 26 years of Greek plenary sessions into a structured, machine-readable corpus for NLP and text mining; and second, the development of this insightful data platform. The data is segmented by topics, time windows, political parties, and speakers to indicate specific periods of activity. Finally, the research employs an interdisciplinary approach involving journalists, policymakers, and social scientists. This approach aims to evaluate and label extractive topics, such as the refugee crisis, using political science methods, comparing these findings with corresponding events in the European Parliament, and applying content analysis techniques like sentiment analysis to gain deeper insights.
