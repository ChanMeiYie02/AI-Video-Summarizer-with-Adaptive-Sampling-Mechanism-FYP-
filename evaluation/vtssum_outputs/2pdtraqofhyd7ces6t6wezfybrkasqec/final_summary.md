# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The presentation introduced the research into the dynamics of RNA in zebrafish, focusing on the role of RNA-binding proteins (RBPs) in disease mechanisms. The discussion centered on how auto-phagy drives disease progression and highlighted the central function of TDP-43 in maintaining protein and RNA homeostasis. TDP-43 is a critical regulator, binding numerous targets to control RNA splicing, stability, and translation, operating in a feedforward loop that links protein and RNA dynamics.

Zebrafish were emphasized as an ideal model for this study due to their genetic characteristics and the ability to rapidly generate pathological models, allowing for quick assessment of phenotypes, such as motor deficits, within just two days of injection. Experimental results demonstrated that reducing TDP-43 function impairs motor function, a effect that can be rescued by co-injecting wild-type TDP-43 RNA. This suggests that the functional interaction between TDP-43 and its RNA occurs at the transcriptional level.

To further advance the field, the research proposed a shift toward studying RNA metabolism in pathology, requiring techniques capable of precisely localizing and co-localizing RNA and proteins within living cells. To achieve this, the team proposed utilizing C2 experiments and fluorescent in situ hybridization (FISH) for single-molecule precision targeting.

To enable this next generation of *in vivo* imaging, a modified CRISPR-Cas9 system was introduced. This system utilizes a "dead Cas9" (dCas9) fused with a fluorescent protein (GFP) and a modified guide RNA to specifically target and visualize RNA. The goal is to express this dCas9-GFP system in zebrafish motor neurons, allowing researchers to visualize specific RNA species as distinct GFP puncta. This integrated genetic and imaging approach will provide a powerful new tool for developing precise *in vivo* methods to target specific transcripts, ultimately offering a more detailed understanding of the central role and interaction of RBPs, such as TDP-43, in disease pathology.
