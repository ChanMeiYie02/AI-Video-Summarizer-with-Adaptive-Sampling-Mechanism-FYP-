# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The lecture introduces the topic of reasoning in knowledge graphs (KGs) using embeddings, starting by defining the Knowledge Graph Completion task, which involves predicting missing relationships. The core objective is to move beyond simple completion to perform complex multi-hop reasoning, such as answering multi-hop queries, path queries, and conjunctive queries, utilizing embeddings to enable logical predictions within the graph structure. The system explores how to effectively embed knowledge graphs and relations to facilitate navigation, using a biomedical knowledge graph as a practical example.

The discussion focuses on using KGs to answer complex queries over incomplete and massive knowledge bases. These queries are categorized into two main types: one-hop queries, which involve simple link prediction, and path queries, which traverse multiple relations to find complex connections. A key challenge addressed is answering these queries when the knowledge graph is incomplete, necessitating methods that predict missing or changing relations rather than relying on explicit traversal.

To tackle this, the speaker discusses knowledge graph completion techniques, starting with simple link prediction (one-hop queries) and generalizing this approach to path queries, where the system must predict the sequence of relations to identify the final entity. However, traditional traversal methods become computationally expensive in incomplete graphs. The proposed solution is "predictive queries," which reframes the task as a prediction problem. This approach allows the system to implicitly impute or complete the missing knowledge, enabling it to answer any path-based query over the incomplete graph. Ultimately, the approach changes the methodology by focusing on predicting the relevant entities that serve as the final answer, thereby generalizing simple link prediction into multi-step reasoning tasks.
