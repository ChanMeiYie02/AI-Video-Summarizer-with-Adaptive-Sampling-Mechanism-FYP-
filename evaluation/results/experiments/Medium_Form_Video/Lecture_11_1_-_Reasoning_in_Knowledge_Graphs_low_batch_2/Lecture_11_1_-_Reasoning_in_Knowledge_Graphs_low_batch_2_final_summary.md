# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The lecture introduces the use of knowledge graphs (KGs) and embeddings to enable complex reasoning and navigation within these structures. The session begins by defining KGs and the task of knowledge graph completion, which involves predicting missing relationships. The main focus then shifts to multi-hop reasoning, covering complex predictions such as Path Queries and Conjunctive Queries, emphasizing how to embed the knowledge graph and its relations to facilitate reasoning. A practical application is established using a biomedical KG structure, including entities like drugs, diseases, and proteins.

The system leverages KGs to answer diverse queries, progressing from simple link prediction (one-hop queries) to complex path queries that traverse multiple relations. A path query is defined by an anchor node and a sequence of relations, which can be graphically represented as a traversal plan. For instance, the system can identify proteins associated with adverse events caused by a drug by following relations like 'causes' and 'associated'.

A major challenge is that KGs are often incomplete, meaning missing relationships can prevent the discovery of relevant entities, making standard traversal methods computationally difficult on massive graphs. To overcome this, the proposed solution is "predictive queries." This method reformulates queries as predictions, allowing the system to implicitly impute or complete the missing parts of the knowledge graph. This approach generalizes link prediction, enabling the system to answer arbitrary path queries over incomplete knowledge graphs and making the method robust to missing information. Ultimately, the task is shifted from a one-step prediction to a multi-step prediction, where the system predicts the entity that serves as the answer to the complex query.
