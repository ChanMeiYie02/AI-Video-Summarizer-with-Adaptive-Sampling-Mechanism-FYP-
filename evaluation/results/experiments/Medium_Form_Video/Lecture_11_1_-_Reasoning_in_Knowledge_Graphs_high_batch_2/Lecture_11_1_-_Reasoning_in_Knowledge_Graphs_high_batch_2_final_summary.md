# Final Multimodal Video Summary

This lecture introduces the use of Knowledge Graphs (KGs) and embeddings for reasoning, focusing on how to perform complex queries and predictions within these structures. Knowledge graphs are defined as structures that capture entity relationships, and the system is designed to handle multi-hop reasoning, including simple one-hop queries and complex path queries, which involve traversing multiple relations in a sequence.

The core challenge addressed is answering these queries over incomplete KGs, as real-world knowledge graphs are often missing crucial relations. To overcome the computational complexity of traditional graph traversal and the risk of missing answers due to missing information, the system proposes a solution centered on knowledge graph completion. Instead of relying solely on explicit traversal, the method introduces "predictive queries," which reformulate path queries as prediction tasks. This approach implicitly imputes missing information to complete the knowledge graph, allowing the system to predict the correct answer to a given query. By generalizing link prediction, this method makes the system robust to incomplete data and enables the efficient answering of arbitrary complex path queries.
