The discussion focuses on two main types of queries:

**AI Summary:**
The discussion focuses on enabling systems to answer queries over incomplete knowledge graphs (KGs).

**Key Concepts:**

1.  **Knowledge Graph Completion (One-Hop Queries):** The system must be able to predict missing links between the head and tail of a relation type $r$. This is equivalent to answering simple link prediction queries, such as finding the side effects caused by a drug.
2.  **Path Queries:** Generalizing the capability to chain multiple relations. A path query $Q$ is defined by a starting entity (anchor node, e.g., `fulvestrant`) and a sequence of relations ($R_1$ to $R_N$).
3.  **Query Plan:** The process involves starting at an entity, traversing through the specified sequence of relations, and predicting the correct final answer.

**Example and Execution:**

The system can be used to answer complex questions, such as: "What proteins are associated with adverse events caused by fulvestrant?"

To find the answer, the system performs a two-step traversal:
1.  It traverses the `causes` relation from `fulvestrant` to identify side effects.
2.  It then traverses the `associated` relation from those side effects to find the relevant proteins, providing the final answer.

**AI Summary:**
The discussion focuses on overcoming the computational hardness of traversing and querying incomplete knowledge graphs (KGs).

The challenge is that simply completing the KG and then traversing it becomes computationally expensive, as the number of possible paths explodes exponentially with query length.

To address this, the proposed method is "predictive queries." This approach aims to answer path-based queries over an incomplete KG by implicitly imputing (completing) the missing knowledge. Instead of explicitly imputing edges and then traversing, the system formulates queries as predictions, allowing it to predict which entities answer a given query. This makes the approach robust to missing information and generalizes the link prediction task. The ultimate goal is to be able to answer any path query at test time.
