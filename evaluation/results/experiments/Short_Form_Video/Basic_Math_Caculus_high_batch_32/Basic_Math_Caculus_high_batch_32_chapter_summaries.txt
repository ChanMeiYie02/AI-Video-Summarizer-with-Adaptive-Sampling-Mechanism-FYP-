**Key Points:**

*   **The Need for Calculus:** Geometry alone is insufficient to find the area beneath complex shapes (like $y=x^2$). Calculus, specifically integration, is necessary.
*   **Method of Integration:** Integration works by approximating the area under a curve by slicing it into numerous, very thin rectangles.
*   **Area Calculation:** To calculate the area, the process involves determining the height (y-value) of each rectangle and multiplying it by its width ($dx$).
*   **Approximation:** As the width of these rectangles ($dx$) becomes infinitesimally small (approaching zero), the sum of the areas of these rectangles provides an excellent approximation of the total area under the parabola.
*   **Formal Definition:** Integration is defined as the summation of these infinitesimal rectangular areas, specifically $\sum (x^2 \cdot dx)$ from $x=0$ to $x=2$.
