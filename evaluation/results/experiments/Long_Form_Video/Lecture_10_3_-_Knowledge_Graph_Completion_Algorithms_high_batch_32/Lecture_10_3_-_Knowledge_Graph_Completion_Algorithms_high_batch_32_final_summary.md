# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The video introduces the field of Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC), focusing on predicting missing information by modeling entities and relations as vectors in an embedding space. The foundational method discussed is TransE, which models a triple (head, relation, tail) by asserting that the sum of the head embedding and the relation vector approximates the tail embedding ($h + r \approx t$).

The discussion then explores the limitations of TransE concerning complex relational properties. While TransE can naturally capture transitivity and inverse relations (by setting the second vector as the negative of the first), it fails to model symmetric relations or one-to-N relationships because it requires distinct entities to map to unique vectors. Furthermore, while some methods can handle composite relations, they often struggle to model the compositionality effectively.

To overcome these limitations, the system introduces advanced methodologies such as ColdTransR and TransAR. These approaches map entities and relations into relation-specific spaces using transformation matrices and projection matrices, allowing the model to learn a unique function for each relation. This mechanism enables the system to successfully model symmetric relations by mapping multiple entities to the same point in the embedding space.

The video further contrasts different scoring functions, comparing simple Euclidean distance measures against bilinear functions that utilize the coordinate-wise product of head, relation, and tail vectors. A bilinear scoring function defines a hyperplane in the embedding space, which can determine if a head-tail relationship is valid. However, the model’s inability to handle inverse relations and the limitations of combining hyperplanes for composite relations remain key constraints.

Finally, the discussion explores complex vector embeddings as an alternative to Euclidean space. By utilizing complex numbers, the model can potentially model symmetric relations or inverse relations. However, it is noted that complex embeddings still cannot adequately model composition or one-to-many relationships. The video concludes by reviewing a diverse landscape of KGC embedding methods, advising that the choice of technique—such as TransE, TransAR, DistMul, or complex models—must be tailored based on the specific type of relationship being modeled.
