# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The video introduces knowledge graph completion (KGC) as a task of predicting the missing end of a knowledge graph triple, given a head node and a relation type. The initial approach leverages shallow node embeddings, aiming to learn embeddings such that the head and relation vector combined approximate the tail vector ($h + r \approx t$). This mechanism allows the model to perform meaningful entity translation. Early methods like TransE learn these embeddings by minimizing the distance between the combined vector and the true tail entity. The discussion explores how these models handle different relation patterns, including symmetric, anti-symmetric, inverse, and composite relations. For instance, Transitivity can capture anti-symmetry, and inverse relations can be modeled by defining the inverse vector as the negative of the original relation vector. However, standard models like TransE struggle with symmetric relationships and one-to-N relations because they force different entities to map to the same embedding point, making them indistinguishable.

To address these limitations, an alternative method called ColdTransR is proposed. ColdTransR separates entities and relations into distinct spaces and uses transformation matrices to map entities into a relation-specific space. This approach allows the model to effectively handle symmetric relations. While ColdTransR solves the symmetry problem, it cannot naturally model the composition of relations, as the different relation-specific spaces are not inherently compositional.

Further exploring advanced modeling techniques, the video introduces complex embeddings. This method embeds entities in a complex vector space, which provides unique capabilities for modeling different relation types. Complex embeddings can successfully model antisymmetric relations and symmetric relations (by setting the imaginary part to zero), and inverse relations (by using the complex conjugate of the relation vector). However, complex embeddings face limitations when trying to model composite relations or one-to-many relationships due to shared scoring functions.

In conclusion, the video compares various knowledge graph embedding methods, including TransE, TransR, DistMult, and Complex. It highlights that the Complex model stands out as the only method capable of modeling composite relations. The optimal choice of a model depends entirely on the relational patterns in the specific knowledge graph, advising users to select the method based on whether symmetric, composite, or complex relations are the primary focus.
