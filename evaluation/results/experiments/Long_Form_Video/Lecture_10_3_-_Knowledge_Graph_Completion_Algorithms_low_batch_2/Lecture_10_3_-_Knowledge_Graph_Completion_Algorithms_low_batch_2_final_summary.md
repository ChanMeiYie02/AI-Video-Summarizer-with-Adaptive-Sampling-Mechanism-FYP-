# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The video introduces knowledge graph completion (KGC) as the task of predicting missing information, specifically identifying the missing tail entity given a head node and a relation type, moving beyond classical link prediction. The proposed methodology utilizes shallow node embeddings rather than Graph Neural Networks, representing entities as triples and aiming to model entities and relations as embedding vectors. The core goal is to enable translation, such that the embedding of the (head, relation) pair approximates the embedding of the tail, following the principle that $E(\text{head}) + E(\text{relation}) \approx E(\text{tail})$. TransE is presented as an initial technique, where the model learns these translation vectors.

Initial discussions highlight the limitations of TransE, noting its inability to model symmetric relations or one-to-N relationships effectively. While transitivity can address inverse and anti-symmetric relations, standard embedding models struggle to capture these complexities. To address these limitations, alternative methods are introduced. ColdTransR and TransAR are proposed, where TransAR utilizes a relation-specific space mapped by a dedicated projection matrix. This approach allows the system to model symmetric relations, handle one-to-N relations, and model inverse relations by mapping entities and relations into a shared space. Although this method is effective, modeling complex composition relations remains challenging.

Furthermore, the video explores alternative scoring functions, such as SRMMet, which uses a bilinear model to capture relationships rather than simple distance metrics. Complex vector embeddings are also demonstrated, allowing for the modeling of symmetric relations (by setting the imaginary part to zero) and inverse relations (by using complex conjugates). However, these complex embeddings still face limitations in effectively modeling composition and one-to-many relations.

In conclusion, the video compares various knowledge graph embedding methods, including TransE, TransH, TransR, and DistMult. It emphasizes that the choice of method depends critically on the relational patterns being predicted. TransR is highlighted as a method capable of modeling composite relations, and it is recommended that TransZ not be used for one-to-N relations. The overall goal is to illustrate the diverse options available for defining predictive models in knowledge graphs based on the specific types of relationships required.
