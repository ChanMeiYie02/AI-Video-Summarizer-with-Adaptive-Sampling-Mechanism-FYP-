# Final Multimodal Video Summary

The video introduces Knowledge Graph Completion (KGC), focusing on methods like TransE, TransR, DistMul, and Complex models, all aimed at predicting a missing entity (tail) given a head node and a specific relation, differentiating this task from classical link prediction. The core methodology involves using shallow node embeddings, where the embedding of the head and the relation should approximate the embedding of the target tail, based on the principle that $head + relation \approx tail$. The foundational approach, TransE, models structured triples $(h, r, t)$ by ensuring this vector approximation holds.

The discussion then explores the expressive limits of these models, focusing on how to handle different types of relationships. Initial challenges arise concerning relation properties, specifically distinguishing between symmetric (reciprocal) and anti-symmetric (one-way) relations. The system is examined for its ability to capture inverse relations (e.g., adviser/advisee) by using the negative of the relation vector, and for composite relations, where the relation vector is a combination of individual relation vectors.

However, standard models like TransE face limitations in handling complex structures, struggling particularly with symmetric relations and one-to-N relationships, as mapping distinct entities to the same embedding point makes them indistinguishable. To overcome this, advanced methods are proposed, such as creating dedicated relation spaces where entities are mapped into a new space and relations are modeled by relation-specific projection matrices.

The system further introduces complex vector embeddings to enhance modeling capabilities. By utilizing complex numbers and the concept of the complex conjugate, these embeddings can effectively model different relation types: symmetric relations can be handled by setting the imaginary part of the relation to zero, and inverse relations can be modeled by using the complex conjugate of one relation vector to represent the inverse.

The video concludes by comparing various embedding methods, noting that while methods operating in real space (like TransE and TransR) are foundational, complex methods offer greater flexibility in defining predictive models. Ultimately, the choice of knowledge graph embedding method depends heavily on the specific relational patterns and properties within a given knowledge graph, emphasizing that models must be tailored to the types of relationships, such as one-to-many, being modeled.
